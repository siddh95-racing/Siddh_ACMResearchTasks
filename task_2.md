# **How To Read Research Paper**

#### **Introduction**

**Research paper** must be read to be in update with current technologies and advances of a particular field. A genuine researcher spends hundreds of hours a year reading the research papers. This is a skill which people really lack but dont feel the need to have it and waste a lot of time. This is about how research papers can be read efficiently with a method "Three-Pass".

#### **Three-Pass Approach**

The author suggests that instead of reading a research paper from beginning to end in detail, we should read it in three passes, with each pass going deeper than the previous one.



#### &#x09;**First Pass:**

The first pass is a quick reading of around 5–10 minutes to get a general idea of the paper. We should read the title, abstract, introduction, section headings, conclusion and briefly look at the references. At the end of this pass, we should be able to answer the Five Cs:



Category – What type of paper is it?

Context – Which other research is it related to?

Correctness– Do its assumptions seem valid?

Contributions – What are the main contributions?

Clarity – Is the paper easy to understand?



Based on this, we can decide whether the paper needs further reading.



#### &#x09;**Second Pass:**

The second pass takes around an hour and focuses on understanding the main content without going deeply into proofs or other difficult details. We should carefully examine figures, diagrams and graphs and check whether the results are presented properly. Important points and relevant references should also be noted. After this pass, we should be able to explain the main idea of the paper to someone else with supporting evidence.



#### &#x09;**Third Pass:**

The third pass is for a deep understanding of the paper, especially when we need to review or critically evaluate it. The main idea is to virtually re-create or re-implement the work ourselves using the authors' assumptions. This helps us identify the paper's strengths, weaknesses, hidden assumptions, missing citations and possible problems in its experiments or analysis. At the end, we should be able to reconstruct the paper's structure and understand it in detail.



#### &#x09;**Literature Survey**

For a literature survey, the author suggests starting with 3–5 recent papers using academic search engines such as Google Scholar and suitable keywords. After getting an overview of these papers, we should examine their related work and references to find commonly cited papers and important researchers. We can then look at the recent publications of these researchers and the proceedings of important conferences in the field. The newly identified papers can be added to the survey, and the process can be repeated whenever important papers are discovered.



#### &#x09;CONCLUSION

The three-pass method helps us avoid getting lost in technical details too early. It allows us to decide how deeply a paper needs to be read based on its importance and our purpose, while saving time and still allowing detailed understanding when necessary.
